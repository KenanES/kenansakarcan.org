KOSDAQ: 051160 · Gaeasoft

Published Nov 20, 2026

South Korea is a market ripe for deep value investors due to ongoing governance reforms and improving structure. Verdad Capital makes the case here.

1. What the business does

Gaeasoft is a Korean company whose main asset is a 54% stake in Oasis Market, the most profitable online grocery delivery company in Korea. Oasis delivers fresh, organic groceries at fair prices and also runs about 50 stores.

Its rivals, Kurly and Coupang’s Rocket Fresh, spent billions on ads and discounts and lost money doing it. Oasis has made a profit every year since it started in 2018.

Its methods are simple. Software plans the packing and delivery routes, so it doesn’t need expensive robot warehouses. Groceries that don’t get delivered are sold in its stores, so it throws away only about 0.18% of its food, compared with 1–4% at competitors. It pays farmers within a week or two instead of a month or two, so good growers keep selling to it. Customers mostly find it through word of mouth, so it spends almost nothing on advertising.

Gaeasoft also owns a smaller software business that earns about ₩8 billion a year.

2. Why it could be a good investment

The stock is extremely cheap. In October, Toss, a big Korean finance app, invested in Oasis at a price that makes Gaeasoft’s stake worth about ₩57,000 for every Gaeasoft share. The stock trades at ₩13,850, so that’s more than four times the price, like a store selling a $100 bill for $25.

The share price barely covers the cash, investments and land Gaeasoft already owns, counting its share of Oasis’s cash, so buyers get the grocery business almost for free. Judged on profits alone, the stock is also cheap at about 9 times earnings.

Management is making good choices. Gaeasoft has bought back and cancelled almost 20% of its shares in two and a half years, partly paid for by selling small pieces of Oasis to private buyers at prices far above what the stock implies. A large US fund, Miri Capital, now owns more than 17% and works with management to raise value for shareholders.

The Toss deal also came with a partnership. Toss has about 8 times as many monthly users as Oasis and is adding an Oasis grocery section to its app. When Naver made a similar deal with Kurly, Kurly’s sales growth jumped from about 10% a year to nearly 30% in under a year.

3. What could go wrong, and why it might still work

The worries: much of the value sits inside Oasis, which has other owners, including investment funds and Toss, with their own goals, so that cash could stay stuck there. Gaeasoft once bought land to make EV batteries, a business it knew nothing about, and it could chase another bad idea with its spare cash.

Oasis bought the bankrupt shopping site Tmon in 2025, and Tmon still hasn’t restarted. Oasis’s sales also fell about 8% last quarter.

The Toss partnership might disappoint, new rules for early-morning delivery could bring more competition, and if Oasis lists its own shares, buyers might pay less than Toss did.

Why it might still work: the sales drop was on purpose. Oasis cut back to focus on profit, and its operating profit rose 86%.

Tmon came with ₩45.7 billion of its own cash, so Oasis only really put in about ₩16 billion. When the relaunch stalled, management stopped spending instead of throwing good money after bad. Toss runs one of Korea’s biggest payment networks, which could help restart Tmon.

The founder owns his stake in Oasis through Gaeasoft (his family owns 34%), so he only wins when Gaeasoft shareholders win.

New Korean laws now require boards to look out for all shareholders, require companies to cancel shares they buy back, and make it harder to list Oasis separately, which makes a buyout of Gaeasoft more likely if Oasis does list.

Even if Oasis is valued far below what investors have paid for it, the stock could still roughly double.
