$HGV · Hilton Grand Vacations

Published Nov 16, 2026

Since publishing: $45.01 at the Nov 16, 2026 close

1. What the business does

Hilton Grand Vacations sells “timeshares.” Instead of buying a whole beach house, a family buys a slice of one and gets a week or so of vacation there every year, for life. Most families can’t pay the roughly $30,000 all at once, so HGV lends them the money at about 15% interest. HGV then borrows that money itself at about 5% and keeps the difference, like a store that makes money on the payment plan as well as the sale. Owners also pay HGV a fee every year to keep the resorts running. Those yearly fees and loan payments keep coming in even when new sales are slow.

2. Why it could be a good investment

The stock is cheap: HGV makes about $8 of extra cash a year for every $44 share. That’s like paying $44 for a lemonade stand that hands you $8 of profit every year. HGV uses that cash to buy back its own shares, about one out of every six shares each year. When there are fewer shares, each one owns a bigger piece of the pie, so each share is worth more even if the pie doesn’t grow. If people stop worrying about the business, the stock could also start trading at a higher price, the way a similar company, Marriott Vacations, roughly doubled this year.

3. What could go wrong, and why it might still work

The worries: HGV’s salespeople sold 11% less this past quarter, so the company now expects to earn less this year. HGV also owes a lot of money, which makes the stock swing harder in both directions. Some customers stop paying back their loans, and the company’s top boss has been selling his own shares.

Why it might still work: about the same number of people are still showing up to sales pitches, so the problem is closing deals, and better training and management can fix that. Marriott Vacations had the same problem and fixed it within a year. Even if HGV never grows, buying back that many shares still makes each remaining share worth more.
